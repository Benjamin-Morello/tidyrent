The landlord's EOFY checklist

Everything to gather before you see the accountant — income, the deduction categories that matter, and the paperwork that goes with them.

Income to gather

  • Total rent received for the financial year (1 July – 30 June) — received, not billed
  • Other rental income: water usage reimbursements, insurance payouts for lost rent, bond money retained

Expense categories your accountant will ask about

  • Repairs & maintenance — fixing damage and wear (distinct from improvements, which are treated differently)
  • Loan interest — interest on the investment loan, not principal
  • Council rates and water charges you paid as owner
  • Landlord and building insurance
  • Strata / body corporate fees
  • Land tax (state-based; thresholds vary)
  • Property management or advertising costs — including software subscriptions and letting fees
  • Gardening, cleaning, pest control
  • Depreciation — if you have a quantity surveyor's schedule, bring it; if the property is newer and you don't, ask your accountant whether one would pay for itself

The documents pile

  • Every receipt and invoice for the categories above
  • Loan statements showing interest charged
  • Rates notices, insurance certificates, strata levies
  • Your rent ledger — a record of what was received and when

The two questions worth asking your accountant

First: is anything I'm calling a repair actually a capital improvement? The tax treatment differs and it's the most common mix-up. Second: am I missing deductions — depreciation schedules and borrowing costs are the ones people forget.
