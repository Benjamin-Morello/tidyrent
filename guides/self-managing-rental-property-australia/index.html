Self-managing your rental: the honest guide

What you're actually signing up for when you skip the agent — and the simple system that keeps it manageable.

What a property manager actually does

For their 5–8% of rent (plus letting and inspection fees), an agent finds and screens tenants, handles the lease and bond, collects rent and chases arrears, coordinates repairs, runs inspections, and keeps you on the right side of your state's tenancy law. Self-managing means taking all of that on yourself. Plenty of people do it well — the honest question is whether you'll do the boring parts consistently.

The parts people underestimate

Compliance. Every state has its own rules on bond lodgement, entry notice periods, minimum standards, and smoke alarms. Your state's tenancy authority (Fair Trading in NSW, Consumer Affairs in VIC, the RTA in QLD) publishes plain-English guides — read yours before the lease is signed, not after a dispute.

Arrears. The awkward conversation is easier at day three than day thirty. Decide your process in advance: when you send a reminder, when it becomes a formal breach notice — and stick to it regardless of how nice the tenant is.

Records. If a dispute ever reaches the tribunal, the landlord with dated records of payments, notices and repairs wins the argument. If it never does, the same records are your tax return. Either way, the ledger is the job.

What self-managers actually need on hand

  • A rent ledger showing what was received and when
  • A folder (or app) of every receipt, categorised for tax
  • Key dates where you'll see them: lease end, inspections, insurance renewal, smoke alarm checks
  • Your state's standard lease, condition report and notice forms
  • Landlord insurance — the one expense not to optimise away

The honest verdict

Self-managing one local property with a good tenant is very doable and saves real money. Self-managing three properties in two states while working full-time is a part-time job. Know which situation you're in, and build the boring system before you need it.
